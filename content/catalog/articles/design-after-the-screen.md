::text{id="screen-opening"}

It's a Thursday a few years from now. A week ago you thought dinner with Ana, and an agent handled the rest: two calendars, one table, one route. You never opened an app.

The distance between your desire and the evening has nearly closed. Part 1 ended with a question: when it does, what's left for designers to do?

The answer starts with this Thursday. A lot still happens between the thought and the table. Almost none of it happens on a screen.

## Less screen, more surface {#screen-surface}

The screen is shrinking as a place to design. The surface for design is growing everywhere else.

That Thursday touches a phone, a watch, earbuds, a car, maybe glasses, the restaurant's own systems and Ana's agent. Several models talk to each other along the way. Each device is a moment where the system meets you, and most of those moments have no screen at all.

Mark Weiser saw this coming in 1991: "The most profound technologies are those that disappear" (Scientific American). The technology disappears. The moments where it meets a person multiply, and each one is designed.

## Designing for the senses {#screen-senses}

At 18:40 a soft tone in your earbuds says it's time to leave. A line of light in your glasses points left at the corner. Your watch taps twice as you reach the door. Each of these lasts a second or two, then it's gone.

This is the new material: sound, light, touch, voice and a glance, arriving for a moment and leaving no trace. Designers now shape transient moments across the senses. That calls for the same craft as before, applied to timing, tone, rhythm and restraint, with a screen as one option among many.

## The delight of "it's handled" {#screen-handled}

Screens spent years telling people they were on the right track: progress bars, step counters, green ticks. In a world with fewer steps, delight moves to affirmation itself, the small pleasure of knowing something is taken care of. Ana's on her way. Your table is by the window. Three words at the right moment can feel better than any confirmation screen.

The rest of the time, the system stays quiet. It's present in the background, carried from device to device as you move through your evening, and it speaks only when speaking helps. Deciding when to stay silent is now one of the most important design calls there is.

## Behaviour is the new material {#screen-behaviour}

When an agent acts for someone, two kinds of decision shape it. Product sets what the system may do. Design shapes how that feels to the person on the receiving end.

::table{id="screen-decisions"}
| Product decides | Design shapes |
| --- | --- |
| Ask when a request is ambiguous | The question itself: one line, one tap, at a moment that doesn't break your day |
| Confirm before committing your evening | How the confirmation sounds in an earbud, looks in glasses, feels on a wrist |
| Allow undo for ten minutes | How undo is offered so it feels safe and calm |
| Keep a record of what the agent did | How you see what happened, and why, at a glance |

::text{id="screen-pixels"}

Pixels were the material of the screen era. Behaviour is the material of this one: timing, tone, interruption and presence.

## Design moves to the destination {#screen-destination}

When getting to the evening takes no effort, the evening itself carries all the weight. The welcome at the door, the table, the way the bill arrives, the walk home. The lived experience at the end of the distance becomes the main stage for design.

That is a far bigger field than screens ever were: services, spaces, objects and real-world moments, joined up by systems nobody sees. Designers who spent twenty years shortening the path now get to shape what's waiting at the end of it.

## Same brief, new canvas {#screen-brief}

The brief stays the one designers have always had: bring a person from what they desire to what they live. The canvas has moved from the screen to the senses, from the path to the destination, from pixels to behaviour.

On Thursday you'll sit down across from Ana, and the evening will feel effortless. Every part of that effortlessness was designed.
